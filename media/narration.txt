A better public space starts with a difficult conversation. Everyone has a different priority. The budget cannot stretch forever. And a beautiful drawing rarely explains what was left out.
This is Common Ground. A place for all of us, with an explanation for every tradeoff.
Here is an illustrative pocket park. We set a budget and minimum commitments to shade, access, play and water. The studio checks all four thousand and ninety-six combinations of twelve interventions. One hundred and forty-five meet every requirement.
A resident can protect a favorite, like inclusive play. The entire plan changes around that commitment. It is still their decision.
Now the budget falls to thirty thousand euros. There is no feasible plan. Common Ground does not quietly drop an accessibility target or invent a result. It shows the exact requirements that conflict.
Then it asks a more useful question. What would have to change? An extra seven thousand euros is enough to satisfy every original minimum. That repair is checked against the complete scenario. We can explore it immediately.
The proposal exports with all its assumptions and a machine-checkable certificate. Everything runs in the browser, and the workshop stays on this device.
This is a working design prototype. Costs and scores are illustrative, and the next step is a facilitated pilot with residents and local design expertise. No field impact is claimed.
Common Ground turns a hidden compromise into a conversation people can actually see.
